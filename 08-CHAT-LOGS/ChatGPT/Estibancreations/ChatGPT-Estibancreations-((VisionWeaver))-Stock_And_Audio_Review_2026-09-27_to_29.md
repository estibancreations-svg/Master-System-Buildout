### What I recommend adding

The catalog should cover footage, photography, generated visuals, music, sound effects, voice assets, titles and graphics, character and environment references, production templates, and approved reusable copy. Each item should distinguish its **source and availability**: owned original, commissioned asset, licensed stock, generated output, searchable external listing, or reference-only material. A search result must never imply publication rights.

An editor should be able to audition or preview candidates, collect them for a scene, and pin an approved *version* to a storyboard shot or timeline. Alongside the media, VisionWeaver should retain its source, receipt, license terms, release documents where applicable, attribution, restrictions, and derivative history. Export checks would then assess the **whole composition** against the intended uses—such as a film, paid ad, book, or social cut—before release.

I also recommend making long-form production visible as a sequence of planned shots, generated or acquired segments, extensions, and an assembled timeline. Show both **target runtime and actual encoded runtime**. That would make it possible to tell whether a requested 10- or 20-minute episode has truly been completed, rather than treating a successful five-second generation as a completed production. Character identity, environment, voice direction, and approved story material should remain versioned across those shots and future episodes.

T.H.E.L.M.A. can dispatch and monitor authorized jobs and surface failures; VisionWeaver should own the creative records, asset placements, reviews, and render history. The CEO Dashboard should receive governed status, costs, approvals, and exceptions. Provider credentials belong in protected server-side configuration, while each output retains its provider, inputs, prompt version, cost, and review result.
