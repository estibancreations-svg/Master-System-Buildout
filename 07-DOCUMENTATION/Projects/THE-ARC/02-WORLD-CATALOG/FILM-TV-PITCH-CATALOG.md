# THE ARC
## Film / Television Franchise Pitch Catalog
### Starship Design Families, World-Building Platform, and Production Bible Outline

**Working pitch position:** *The Arc* is not one spaceship. It is a family of civilization-scale artificial worlds whose engineering, ecology, architecture and daily life are treated with enough internal consistency that the audience can learn the rules of the setting the same way they learn the geography of a city.

The franchise hook is simple: **these vessels do not merely transport people; they carry complete worlds.** Each class solves the same problem differently—how to move civilization, water, atmosphere, food, culture, industry and life through space without reducing the setting to hallways and control rooms.

The catalog is designed to serve four jobs at once:

1. **Studio pitch material** — clear visual and story differentiation between ship classes.
2. **Production design bible** — fixed design rules for sets, VFX, props, environments and animation.
3. **Entity catalog** — every person, animal, plant, material, machine, district and utility can be assigned a record.
4. **Reality model** — every record can later receive mass, volume, power, water, thermal, maintenance and population values so the fictional world can be costed and engineered as if it were real.

---

# I. EXECUTIVE PITCH OUTLINE

## 1. The One-Sentence Hook
A network of enormous artificial worlds carries human civilization through space, but every ship is a living political, ecological and mechanical system whose failures can threaten millions.

## 2. Format Options
- **Prestige one-hour drama:** one flagship Arc, recurring districts, deep family/political arcs, major engineering events.
- **Limited series:** a single voyage, crisis or transformation event across one class.
- **Anthology:** each season follows a different Arc class and society.
- **Feature film:** one high-consequence event with the vessel itself functioning as a principal character.
- **Shared-universe franchise:** multiple Arc classes, fleet politics, migration, trade, environmental specializations and cultural divergence.

## 3. The Promise to the Audience
The audience should be able to recognize where they are from geometry alone. A branch, ring, shield track, ocean lane, civic district, service deck or under-panel ecosystem should have a consistent identity. The setting rewards attention because geography, ecology and engineering matter to the plot.

## 4. Visual Thesis
- Vast lived-in curvature instead of corridor-only science fiction.
- Architecture integrated with mechanical structure rather than pasted onto it.
- Visible transportation, water, agriculture, power, maintenance and waste systems.
- Interior horizons that place cities and landscapes overhead.
- Exterior silhouettes that remain class-specific.
- Transformations caused by mechanisms, not unexplained morphing.
- Human-scale streets and homes contrasted against kilometer-scale infrastructure.
- Water, vegetation and atmosphere treated as infrastructure as well as scenery.

## 5. Dramatic Engines
- Resource allocation
- Water rights and ecological governance
- Branch identity and class geography
- Ring/core access
- Agricultural failures
- Habitat expansion
- Shield closure and pressure events
- Thermal crises
- Transportation shutdowns
- Marine ecosystem imbalance
- Political conflict between branches
- Migration between ship classes
- Maintenance labor and invisible infrastructure
- Fleet-wide emergencies
- Generational memory and cultural divergence
- The moral question of what must be preserved when a civilization becomes portable

# II. STARSHIP CATALOG

## ARC-01 — Passover-Class Master Arc

**Role in the franchise:** Canonical production base and primary screen-world vessel.

**Visual hook:** A colossal inhabited rotating world wrapped around an axial transit spine: city, sky, water, parks, megastructure, and civilization in one impossible interior vista.

**Story engine:** Best for the flagship setting, political centers, migration stories, disasters, ecological conflict, class geography, long-form family stories, and recurring episodic locations.

**Production lock:** Build from WORLD_01_MASTER and the first Passover video. Five branches in the locked design logic, squared ends, protected ring/spine geometry, four stabilizing quadrants, centered antenna/spire when visible, no substitution with the later centered radial reference vessel.

# III. SEASON / FEATURE STORY STRUCTURE OPTIONS

## A. Flagship-series structure
A flagship Arc is treated as a city, country and spaceship simultaneously. Episodes can move between:
- family life on a branch;
- branch-level government;
- core transit;
- utility workers beneath the habitat;
- marine biologists in under-panel oceans;
- agricultural managers on green shields;
- engineers in ring service structures;
- pilots and freight crews;
- medical and ecological emergency teams.

## B. Five-branch dramatic map
The five branches can support distinct cultural identities without becoming five unrelated planets. Their differences may come from:
- land-use mix;
- density;
- branch age;
- immigration history;
- primary industries;
- ecology;
- political structure;
- architectural eras;
- resource access;
- proximity to critical infrastructure.

## C. Signature set pieces
1. First interior reveal of the curved inhabited world.
2. Flight along the axial spine.
3. Passage through the central rings.
4. Exterior reveal of the five-branch silhouette.
5. Shield doors beginning clockwise closure.
6. Pressure-lock sequence.
7. Green ecological activation after lock.
8. Controlled water deployment across designated surfaces.
9. Water entering top and bottom ring lanes.
10. Under-panel ocean reveal.
11. Mass transit shutdown inside a branch.
12. Core/spire communications event.
13. Branch evacuation.
14. Thermal overload visible through water and radiator behavior.
15. Civilian street scene with an inhabited horizon overhead.

# IV. PRODUCTION BIBLE OUTLINE

## 1. Geometry Bible
Every class requires orthographic plan, elevation, section, axial view, branch section, ring section, shield track detail and human-scale reference.

## 2. Environment Bible
Each named district receives:
- geographic coordinates;
- gravity vector;
- local horizon;
- climate;
- architecture;
- infrastructure;
- population;
- primary colors/materials;
- transit links;
- water access;
- emergency routes;
- day/night behavior.

## 3. Biology Bible
Every organism shown on screen receives an entity record covering:
- common and scientific name;
- functional role;
- habitat;
- diet/nutrients;
- water/temperature/salinity requirements;
- population scale;
- reproduction;
- disease controls;
- genetic preservation;
- interactions with other species.

## 4. Material Bible
Every visible structural or manufactured material receives:
- material class;
- density;
- mechanical role;
- corrosion behavior;
- temperature range;
- radiation/shielding role;
- repair method;
- recyclability;
- visual finish;
- where it appears.

## 5. Motion Bible
Every moving megastructure component receives:
- path;
- speed range;
- inertia;
- lock points;
- safety clearances;
- emergency stop behavior;
- visual indicators;
- sound;
- maintenance access.

## 6. Water Bible
Water is divided into separate inventories:
- potable;
- hygiene/service;
- agriculture;
- firefighting;
- industrial;
- thermal exchange;
- weather/climate;
- aquatic habitat;
- reserve/ballast;
- waste-water/reclamation.

## 7. Continuity Bible
No visual generation, set change or animation is approved without verifying:
- correct vessel class;
- correct branch count;
- correct branch termini;
- correct ring/core geometry;
- correct quadrant structures;
- correct antenna/spire state;
- correct water zone;
- correct gravity orientation;
- correct ecology;
- correct architectural era and district identity.

# V. WHY THE REALITY CATALOG MATTERS

The production advantage is that **story, VFX and engineering all reference the same entity registry**. If a dolphin appears in a Pelagic under-panel ocean, the registry can tell production what habitat it belongs to, what other species share that water, whether the water is marine or fresh, which scenes can plausibly reuse the same biome, and what the real system would need to support it.

The same logic applies to steel, soil, oxygen, elevators, roads, power cables, trees, emergency doors, apartments, pumps and rings. This prevents the common science-fiction problem where the environment changes to serve a shot and then contradicts itself later.

# VII. FRANCHISE POSITION

The Arc should be pitched as **a reusable world platform**, not a single spectacle. Once the geometry and entity registry are locked, the production can generate new districts, stories, interiors, exterior angles and operational states without losing continuity. The engineering detail is not there merely to prove plausibility; it creates plot, culture, jobs, conflict and visual identity.
