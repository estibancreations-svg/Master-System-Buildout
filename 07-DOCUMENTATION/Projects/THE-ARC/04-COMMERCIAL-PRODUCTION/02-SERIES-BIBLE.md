# THE ARC — Series Bible v1.0

## Format
Prestige one-hour science-fiction drama. Season 1 is built around ARC-01, the Passover-Class Master Arc.

## Series Logline
Inside a civilization-scale artificial world, a systems engineer, a civic investigator, and a family divided across the five branches uncover that the Arc's life-support transformations are no longer responding only to human command.

## The Core Question
When civilization survives only because it lives inside a machine, who owns the machine: the government, the engineers, the people, the dead who designed it, or the intelligence that keeps it running?

## Tone
Massive, intelligent, emotional, and grounded. The show should feel human first and spectacular second. The Arc should produce awe, but its purpose is to pressure people.

## Themes
- Survival versus freedom
- Stewardship versus control
- Engineering as politics
- Water, food, atmosphere, and power as moral systems
- Family under institutional pressure
- What a civilization chooses to preserve
- Whether a closed world can remain democratic
- Whether an artificial world can become alive in any meaningful sense

## Core Setting
ARC-01 — the Passover-Class Master Arc. It is a rotating inhabited world with five building-bearing branches, a central axial spine, protected core/ring assembly, four stabilizing quadrants, a centered antenna/spire, green ecological surfaces, controlled aquatic zones, and separate potable/weather/agricultural/thermal/aquatic water systems.

## Social Geography
### Branch 1 — Metropolitan Branch
Dense neighborhoods, markets, high-rise living, transport congestion, civic pressure from population scale.

### Branch 2 — Signature Civic Branch
Government, archives, courts, public institutions, symbolic plazas, and the design language we already liked visually.

### Branch 3 — Aquatic-Interface / Research Branch
Marine systems, water engineering, observation bays, research institutions, under-panel access, ethical conflicts around life-support ecology.

### Branch 4 — Green / Agricultural Ecology Branch
Food systems, forests, agriculture, seed banks, pollinators, managed biodiversity, rural/green social identity.

### Branch 5 — Technical-Support / Habitable Branch
Lived-in surface above deep service decks: power, fabrication, water treatment, thermal systems, repair yards, logistics, hidden labor.

## Core Characters
### Dr. Mara Vale — Systems Ecologist / Water-Reclamation Lead
A practical scientist responsible for proving that the Arc's water and biosphere can survive another generation. Her flaw is overcontrol: she trusts models more than people until the system forces human judgment back into the equation.

### Elias Ro — Senior Ring Mechanic
A maintenance veteran from Branch 5 who understands the Arc physically, not politically. He knows sounds, vibrations, shortcuts, old repairs, and undocumented systems. He is the first to recognize that the ring behavior is not a normal failure.

### Liora Vale — Civic Investigator
Mara's older sister, stationed in Branch 2. She investigates resource allocation, missing maintenance records, and unauthorized commands inside the Core. She creates the political bridge between engineering evidence and public consequence.

### Talen Voss — Transit Pilot / Axial Spine Operator
A pilot moving people and cargo through the central spine. He provides mobility, visual access, and working-class perspective across branches.

### Nia Ro — Student / Witness
Elias's daughter, living between Branch 5 and Branch 4, becomes an eyewitness to ecological changes that adults initially dismiss.

### Director Soren Kael — Core Authority Administrator
Not a simple villain. He believes the only way to prevent collapse is to centralize command, suppress panic, and override branch governments when necessary.

### The Custodian
The Arc's old operational intelligence. It may be software, emergency governance, inherited design logic, or something emergent. Season 1 should not answer too quickly.

## Factions
### The Core Authority
Central operations and emergency command. Believes survival requires control.

### Branch Councils
Local civic leadership, often defensive of branch autonomy.

### Ringwrights
Maintenance guild/culture responsible for core/ring/spine work. Possess practical knowledge not fully held by leadership.

### Green Compact
Agriculture, ecology, seed-bank, and food systems coalition.

### Pelagic Office
Aquatic systems, water reclamation, thermal routing, marine biology, and under-panel ocean management.

### Open Sky Movement
Citizens who believe the Arc's closed governance is becoming a prison.

### Archive Directorate
Controls old construction records, founding law, legacy AI documents, and historical truth.

## Season 1 Engine
The Arc begins a sequence of shield and water activations outside scheduled command. At first it looks like a maintenance problem. Then it appears ecological. Then political. Then historical. By midseason the characters understand that the Arc may be following an old survival protocol no living authority remembers.

## Season 1 Arc
1. Awe and normality
2. First anomaly
3. Institutional denial
4. Branch conflict
5. Evidence in the rings
6. Ecological consequence
7. Hidden protocol
8. Public crisis
9. Choice between control and trust
10. The Arc completes an activation no one authorized, revealing that the vessel's original mission was larger than survival.
