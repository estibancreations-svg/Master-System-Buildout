# THE ARC — Business and Revenue Plan v1.0

## Business Objective
Turn The Arc into a commercial entertainment property with sellable creative assets, controlled IP documentation, proof-of-concept media, and scalable revenue paths.

## Entity to Build
Create a dedicated production/IP vehicle for the property. The exact legal structure should be reviewed with an attorney and accountant, but operationally the project needs:
- IP ownership ledger
- asset provenance log
- contributor agreements
- production budget
- development budget
- pitch deck
- proof-of-concept trailer
- screenplay/treatment
- investor/producer package

## Revenue Paths
### Primary
- option/sale/license of film or television rights
- producer attachment and packaging
- studio/streamer development deal
- independent proof-of-concept financing

### Secondary
- novels
- graphic novel
- art book
- technical world book
- short films
- YouTube/behind-the-scenes worldbuilding content
- collectible art/posters

### Long-term
- game rights
- VR/interactive exploration
- educational speculative-engineering content
- model kits / collectibles
- licensed worldbuilding platform
- VisionWeaver/Digital Twin production tooling

## First Commercial Product
The first commercial product should not be the entire show. It should be a **pitchable package**:
1. pitch deck
2. pilot treatment
3. series bible
4. character/faction bible
5. 90-second trailer
6. visual development packet
7. budget workbook
8. rights/provenance packet

## Money Strategy
### Low-cost path
Use AI-assisted development and proof-of-concept production to create a professional-looking package before spending on a full pilot.

### Mid-cost path
Produce a polished short proof-of-concept scene or 3–5 minute proof-of-concept short.

### High-cost path
Attach producers, raise financing and build a full pilot presentation.

## What Must Be Avoided
- spending heavily before chain-of-title is clean
- pitching a concept with no story
- presenting too much engineering material before the emotional hook
- generating random assets without registry IDs
- replacing the canonical design without approval
- promising technical production capability that has not been tested
